What the study actually did

Gilmore, Klimek, Abrahamsson and Baar, Sports Medicine – Open 2024. A retrospective review of 526 climbers who logged training in Crimpd and completed two strength tests within four to sixteen weeks.

Abrahangs: an 18–22 mm edge, feet on the floor, ten-second holds with twenty seconds of rest, twenty reps across six grips. The instruction was to feel only slight tension in the forearm, described as roughly forty percent of max. At least six hours between sessions. Max hangs: 20 mm, six reps of ten seconds at 85–95 percent of 1RM, two minutes of rest. The strength test defined 1RM as the weight you could hold for seven seconds.

The results

Climbing alone produced no change. Abrahangs alone gained 2.5 percent on average, but was not statistically significant against the control group. Max hangs alone gained 3.2 percent, significant. Both together gained 5.8 percent, significantly better than either on its own. Effect sizes 0.29, 0.45 and 0.79.

What we don't know

No randomisation, self-selected programs, no control over other training. Above all, the load in the Abrahangs group was never measured — the authors write that those who saw an effect may simply have pulled harder than the rest. That is the gap a Progressor closes.

The study also says nothing about injured fingers. None of the participants were followed up for injuries, and the rehab protocol in this app is built on general principles of load progression, not on data. It is no substitute for a physiotherapist who gets to look at the finger.

About ten millimetres

Neither the study nor Emil's original went below 18 mm for submaximal work. The same force on a 10 mm edge means higher pressure per unit area against the tendon sheaths. 20 mm is what has been tested.

How the targets are calculated

The percentage is based on your measured half-crimp hold strength on the selected edge. For grips with fewer fingers the target is lowered according to Emil's own effort levels: three-finger drag at the same level as four, front two open at around seventy percent, two-finger crimp at around forty-five. The factors are a translation, not something that has been measured.
